What-if before you sign: scenario modeling for your SAP renewal

Renewal is the one moment when your FUE count is cheap to change. Most teams walk in with the count they have and a hope that next year's clean-up will earn a credit. Walk in with a number you have already modeled instead.

Oct 1, 20266 min readBy the LicenseIQ team

The renewal trap

You renew what you have because you do not know what you need. The vendor prices the current count, because that is the only number in the room. Any optimization you do afterwards becomes a credit against a future true-up, on the vendor's terms, if you remember to claim it. The cycle repeats every term, and the gap between what is licensed and what is used quietly widens.

Breaking the cycle means knowing, before the negotiation, what your estate would cost after the changes you are prepared to make. That is a modeling problem, and it should be solved on paper first.

Model, don't guess

A scenario is a set of proposed changes to job profiles: remove role X from profile P, or replace an Advanced-classified role with its Core-classified equivalent. For every job profile the model shows roles by tier before and after, FUE before and after, and the annual cost change at your price. Add the rows up and you have the number you can commit to, with the list of changes that produce it.

Reference scenario. Removing a maintain-type role from three job profiles changed the bill by $864, $432 and $432 a year at $540 per FUE; the other 4,756 profiles were untouched. Small on purpose: a dry run proves the mechanics before you scale it. The same change applied across forty profiles is a very different negotiation.

Because the model runs on exported data, nothing in production changes until you decide it should. You can test a redesign, reject it, and test another in an afternoon.

Consolidation keeps the model honest

Fewer job profiles means fewer things to classify, review and defend, and a scenario that holds after go-live.

Role sprawl is the enemy of any scenario. If two hundred profiles are near-copies of each other, a change you model on one has to be repeated on the rest, and the first one you miss re-creates the problem. In the reference assessment, 109,679 of the 114,228 candidate profile pairs above an 80% similarity threshold were identical in their entitlements: one profile could have served both. Consolidating them first makes every scenario smaller, and makes the resulting FUE count stable.

A renewal checklist

  • Ninety days out. Run the assessment. Agree the inactivity definition with the business and record it.
  • Sixty days out. Validate the idle cohort with HR. Model two or three scenarios and pick the one you will actually commit to, with its change list.
  • Thirty days out. Execute the low-risk removals through your change process. Re-run. The new count is your opening position.
  • At the table. Price in FUE. Keep the per-user evidence to hand. Negotiate the count you have proven, not the one you inherited.

None of this requires a new tool to be installed in SAP, or anyone to be given new access. It requires the exports you already have, a model you trust, and a decision about what "unused" means, made before the vendor makes it for you.
